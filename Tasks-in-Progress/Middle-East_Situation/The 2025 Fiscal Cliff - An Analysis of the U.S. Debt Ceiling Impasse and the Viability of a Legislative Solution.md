# **The 2025 Fiscal Cliff: An Analysis of the U.S. Debt Ceiling Impasse and the Viability of a Legislative Solution**

## **Chapter 1: A Convergence of Crises - America's Fiscal Trials in 2025**

In 2025, the United States faces not a single fiscal problem, but a "convergence of crises" where multiple critical deadlines arrive simultaneously. The debt ceiling issue is merely the flashpoint in this complex situation. This chapter details the elements constituting this brinkmanship—the reinstatement of the debt ceiling, the looming "X-Date" when a default becomes a real possibility, and other fiscal deadlines constraining congressional action—to clarify the high-stakes nature of the 2025 fiscal debate.

### **1.1 When the Clock Resets: The Return of the Debt Ceiling**

The Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023, enacted on June 3, 2023, temporarily resolved the previous debt ceiling standoff by suspending the limit until January 1, 2025.¹ This measure averted a political showdown during the 2024 presidential election year but merely postponed the problem.

On January 2, 2025, as stipulated by the law, the debt ceiling was reinstated. The new limit was set at the outstanding debt level of the previous day, $36.1 trillion.¹ Immediately after, the redemption of securities held by the Medicare trust fund reduced the debt by $54 billion, creating a brief window of borrowing capacity for the Treasury.¹ However, this was only a minor reprieve. On January 21, the Treasury began employing "extraordinary measures"—accounting tools to avoid breaching the debt limit.¹ These measures include temporarily suspending investments in certain government retirement funds, such as the Civil Service Retirement and Disability Fund (CSRDF) and the Postal Service Retiree Health Benefits Fund (PSRHBF).¹ This creates a months-long grace period for Congress to pass legislation to raise the debt ceiling.

### **1.2 The "X-Date": Pinpointing the Precipice**

The "X-Date" is a financial market term for the day on which the Treasury's extraordinary measures and cash on hand are fully exhausted, leaving the government unable to meet all its payment obligations on time.¹⁰ After this date, the U.S. would either face its first-ever sovereign default or be forced to delay payments such as Social Security benefits and federal employee salaries.

Multiple expert organizations have released forecasts for the 2025 X-Date, though their timelines vary.

- **Congressional Budget Office (CBO) Forecast:** August or September 2025.¹ The CBO analyzes this as the most likely period.
    
- **Bipartisan Policy Center (BPC) Forecast:** Mid-July to early October 2025.¹⁴ The BPC's slightly wider range reflects the high level of uncertainty.
    
- **Treasury Department (Secretary Scott Bessent) View:** Has indicated the possibility of funds being exhausted in August and has urged Congress to act by July.¹⁶
    

The primary reason for this variance is the uncertainty surrounding the volume of tax revenues collected around the April 15 filing deadline. The CBO has warned that if tax receipts fall significantly below projections, the X-Date could be pulled forward to late May or June.¹ This uncertainty surrounding the X-Date itself becomes a tool for political maneuvering, pressuring Congress to act sooner rather than later.

### **1.3 The Legislative Gauntlet: A Year of Converging Fiscal Deadlines**

The 2025 debt ceiling problem is particularly severe because it is not an isolated issue. Congress must simultaneously address several other critical fiscal deadlines, and these issues are intricately linked. This situation encourages Congress to attempt a comprehensive solution via a single large bill, but it also dramatically increases the difficulty of negotiations.

- **March 14:** A short-term government funding measure (continuing resolution) expires.³
    
- **September 30:** The end of Fiscal Year 2025. This brings the expiration of the spending caps set by the Fiscal Responsibility Act, as well as the authorization for numerous programs, including Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), various Medicare and Medicaid provisions, and the Farm Bill.³
    
- **December 31:** The "fiscal cliff," when the individual income tax cuts from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) and the enhanced premium subsidies from the Affordable Care Act (ACA) are set to expire.³
    

This concentration of deadlines threatens to paralyze the normal legislative process. With insufficient time to address each issue individually, a dynamic emerges that pushes for these challenges to be bundled into a single massive omnibus bill (a so-called "megabill"). While efficient if successful, this approach maximizes the risk of failure. If this megabill collapses, it could trigger a "systemic policy failure," with a government shutdown, a debt default, and massive tax increases occurring simultaneously. This is a far more serious situation than past debt ceiling confrontations.

Analyzing this situation reveals that the 2025 debt ceiling crisis is less about debt itself and more about taxation. The Republican strategy is to use the catastrophic threat of the summer X-Date as leverage to force the passage of a completely separate policy priority: the permanent extension of the TCJA tax cuts, which are otherwise set to expire at year's end. This is a strategic manipulation of the legislative calendar, using the debt ceiling as a legislative hostage. By linking the non-negotiable urgency of the X-Date to the TCJA extension—a matter that would normally require more deliberate debate—Republicans aim to achieve their policy objectives.

## **Chapter 2: The Republican Gamble - The "One Big Beautiful Bill" and Budget Reconciliation**

In 2025, with control of the White House and both chambers of Congress, the Republican Party has chosen a powerful legislative procedure to resolve the debt ceiling issue without Democratic cooperation: Budget Reconciliation. This chapter provides a detailed analysis of this Republican strategy, the content of the core "megabill," and the vulnerabilities inherent in this approach.

### **2.1 The Chosen Weapon: Budget Reconciliation**

Budget reconciliation is a special legislative process that allows for the passage of budget-related bills in the Senate with a simple majority of 51 votes, bypassing the 60-vote threshold typically needed to overcome a filibuster.² Republicans aim to use this procedure to package tax cuts, spending reductions, and a debt ceiling increase into a single bill, thereby circumventing Democratic opposition.

The first step in this process is for both the House and Senate to pass a budget resolution. This resolution includes "instructions" directing various committees on the target amounts for spending cuts or tax reductions to be included in the reconciliation bill.²² However, this procedure has limitations. Only budget-related items can be included, and the "Byrd Rule" in the Senate restricts the inclusion of non-budgetary policies.²⁵ Furthermore, concerning the debt ceiling, this process cannot "suspend" the limit to a future date; it must be "raised" to a specific dollar amount.¹⁸

### **2.2 Contents of the "Megabill": Tax Cuts and Spending Reductions**

The comprehensive bill sought by Republicans, often referred to as the "megabill" or what President Trump calls the "One Big Beautiful Bill," is primarily composed of three elements.

- **Tax Policy:** The centerpiece of the bill is the permanent extension of the individual income tax cuts from the TCJA, set to expire at the end of 2025.³ Analyses by organizations like the Tax Foundation estimate that extending these cuts would reduce federal revenue by $4 trillion to $4.5 trillion over the next decade.²²
    
- **Spending Policy:** To partially offset the revenue loss from tax cuts, large-scale spending reductions are planned. House Republicans aim to cut federal spending by $1.7 trillion to $2.5 trillion.³ The main targets for these cuts are Medicaid (including the introduction of new work requirements), education, and other non-defense discretionary spending.²⁴ President Trump's budget proposal also calls for a 22.6% cut in non-defense discretionary spending.²⁸
    
- **Debt Ceiling:** The House budget plan includes instructions to raise the debt ceiling by $4 trillion, from its current level of $36.1 trillion to $40.1 trillion.¹⁹
    

This Republican strategy is essentially a product of intra-party negotiation, not an attempt at bipartisan governance. The bill is a container filled with policy "sweeteners" designed to satisfy various Republican factions (fiscal hawks, tax-cut proponents, SALT-state members, etc.) and is designed to completely exclude Democrats. Its success depends on the ability of the Republican leadership to quell internal rebellion. If one element is changed or removed, it could provoke other factions, risking the collapse of the entire bill like a house of cards.

### **2.3 Fiscal Contradictions: CBO Analysis vs. Political Rhetoric**

While Republicans claim this bill will restore fiscal discipline, an analysis by the non-partisan Congressional Budget Office (CBO) directly contradicts this assertion. The CBO has projected that making the TCJA tax cuts permanent, even with spending cuts, would increase the fiscal deficit by trillions of dollars and cause the public debt to exceed 200% of GDP by 2054.¹⁵

In response to this stark CBO analysis, Republicans have countered using an accounting method known as the "current policy baseline."¹⁵ This method assumes that the tax cuts will be extended as a matter of course and does not count the resulting revenue loss as a cost. Using this approach, the tax cuts appear to have "zero cost," and the spending cuts seem to translate directly into deficit reduction. However, this method is incompatible with the CBO's standard analytical practices and creates a fundamental disconnect between the political message of fiscal responsibility and the economic reality of the bill. This is a high-risk rhetorical strategy that abandons the foundation of objective analysis and undermines the credibility of their entire fiscal framework.

### **2.4 Internal Divisions: The Vulnerability of a Slim Majority**

Although Republicans control all key branches of government, their success is not guaranteed. Because their majorities in both the House and Senate are razor-thin, the bill could easily be defeated by just a few defections from within the party.⁹

- **Fiscal Hawks:** There is a conservative faction within the Republican party that opposes any bill that significantly increases the national debt, regardless of the spending cuts. They may vote against the bill if they deem it does not sufficiently reduce the deficit.³⁰
    
- **The SALT Caucus:** Republican members from high-tax states like New York are demanding a significant increase in the cap on the state and local tax (SALT) deduction, currently at $10,000.²⁵ Meeting this demand would further increase the cost of the bill, inviting backlash from other Republicans.
    
- **External Pressure:** Prominent business figures like Elon Musk have publicly criticized the bill for increasing the deficit, adding pressure on fiscal hawks.²⁵
    

These internal conflicts pose a formidable challenge for the Republican leadership. The seemingly monolithic strategy of a reconciliation bill is actually built on these fragile points of unity, with the constant risk that a single crack could lead to a total collapse.

## **Chapter 3: The Democratic Defense - A Stance of "Responsible Governance" and Strategic Patience**

As Republicans pursue a unilateral path focused on party unity through budget reconciliation, the Democratic Party has deployed a different strategy under the banner of "responsible governance." Their approach is based on "strategic patience," waiting for Republican internal contradictions to become apparent and seizing the opportunity to take control of the negotiations. This chapter analyzes the Democratic counter-strategy, its messaging, and its long-term policy goals for the debt ceiling issue.

### **3.1 The Core Position: A "Clean" Bill and No Hostage-Taking**

Democrats have consistently demanded the passage of a "clean" bill to raise the debt ceiling, with no preconditions attached, arguing that it is a basic duty of governance to cover spending previously approved by Congress.⁶ They have harshly condemned the Republican strategy of using spending cuts as a bargaining chip, labeling it "brinkmanship" and "hostage-taking" that endangers the global economy.⁶ This clear stance is intended to frame the Republican tactics as ethically problematic even before negotiations begin.

### **3.2 The Counter-Narrative: Emphasizing Economic Risk and Republican Hypocrisy**

The Democratic counter-strategy is based on constructing a powerful narrative with two pillars.

- **Warning of Economic Catastrophe:** Democrats have repeatedly highlighted the devastating consequences of a default, citing analyses from expert bodies like Moody's and the CBO. They appeal to public anxiety by citing specific figures, such as a severe recession, millions of job losses, and the evaporation of trillions of dollars in household wealth.⁵ This is a strategy to frame the debt ceiling issue not as a partisan squabble but as an economic crisis that directly affects the lives of ordinary citizens.
    
- **Exposing Fiscal Hypocrisy:** Democrats have sharply attacked the contradictions in the Republican claims of fiscal discipline. They point out that the biggest driver of future deficits is the permanent extension of the TCJA tax cuts promoted by Republicans,³² and they highlight the fact that Republicans raised the debt ceiling without issue under the Trump administration.⁶ This "hypocrisy" campaign aims to peel away support for the Republicans from non-partisan and moderate voters who prioritize fiscal issues.
    

### **3.3 The Strategy of Patience: Waiting for a Republican Failure**

With Republicans holding all levers of government, the legal responsibility for resolving the debt ceiling issue lies with them.³⁰ Leveraging this situation to their full advantage, Democrats are strategically waiting for the Republican reconciliation bill to self-destruct due to internal conflict, rather than proactively offering counter-proposals.³⁰

This strategy is based on an accurate assessment of Republican vulnerabilities. Democrats are well aware that the Republicans have a slim majority and are divided by fiscal hawks and the SALT caucus. Therefore, their most powerful political position is to simply say "no" to the Republican partisan plan. By refusing to negotiate on Republican terms, they force the GOP to confront its own internal contradictions.

If the Republican attempt fails and the X-Date draws near, the GOP will be forced to seek Democratic votes to avoid a default. At that moment, the Democrats' negotiating power will be maximized. The price for their cooperation will likely be the removal of most of the partisan policies from the bill and an agreement to a "cleaner" increase (or suspension) of the debt ceiling that lasts until after the 2026 midterm elections.¹⁸

### **3.4 The Long-Term Goal: Reform or Abolition of the Debt Ceiling**

To prevent similar crises in the future, Democrats are increasingly advocating for a permanent solution to the debt ceiling system itself.

One proposed radical solution is to abolish the debt ceiling entirely.⁶ The argument is that with the U.S. dollar as the world's reserve currency, such an artificial limit is an anachronism, and Congress has sufficient oversight authority through the normal budget process.

A more moderate proposal is to reform the system. For example, the "Debt Ceiling Reform Act" proposes a mechanism whereby the Treasury Secretary could continue to pay the nation's bills, and Congress would have to pass a resolution of disapproval to stop it. Interestingly, this mechanism was once proposed by Republican Senator Mitch McConnell to resolve the 2011 crisis.⁶

What emerges from this analysis is that the Fiscal Responsibility Act⁵ that resolved the 2023 debt ceiling crisis, though an imperfect model, is likely a good indicator of the outcome of the 2025 crisis. That agreement was a bipartisan compromise born out of a divided government. In 2025, even with Republicans in full control, a similar outcome—a crisis-averting bipartisan compromise—is likely if their partisan attempt fails. This suggests that the debt ceiling system, due to the catastrophic consequences of its failure, ultimately forces a self-preservationist, bipartisan response from both parties. When pushed to the brink, the political system "defaults" to self-preservation.

## **Chapter 4: The Trump Variable - Presidential Influence and Strategic Ambiguity**

In the 2025 debt ceiling negotiations, President Donald Trump's presence serves as a highly unpredictable "variable." His inconsistent statements and actions complicate the Republican strategy and introduce significant uncertainty into the outcome of the negotiations. This chapter analyzes President Trump's unique role from the perspectives of his historical rhetoric, current stance, and influence on the Republican leadership.

### **4.1 A History of Contradiction**

President Trump's stance on the debt ceiling has changed dramatically depending on his position. Before his presidency and under Democratic administrations, he repeatedly urged Republicans to use the debt ceiling as a powerful negotiating "lever."³¹ This aligns with the traditional view of conservative Republicans who see the debt ceiling as a political weapon.

However, when he faced the debt ceiling deadline as president himself, his attitude shifted entirely. He has repeatedly called for the complete elimination of the debt ceiling, describing it as a "destructive" tool that could lead to economic catastrophe.¹⁸ This reversal is not just a whim; it reflects the change in his political position from "negotiator" to "governing official."

### **4.2 An Unlikely Alliance: Trump and Warren**

One of the most surprising developments in the 2025 negotiations was President Trump's public agreement on his social media platform, Truth Social, with the progressive firebrand Senator Elizabeth Warren (D) on her call to abolish the debt ceiling.¹⁸ He quoted Warren's post and stated, "After many years, I am very pleased to announce that I can agree with Senator Elizabeth Warren on something," and called for bipartisan cooperation on the issue.

This action directly undermined the strategy of his own Republican leadership, who were pursuing a debt ceiling "increase" as a central pillar of their reconciliation bill. This handed the Democrats a powerful talking point: "Even President Trump acknowledges the dangers of the Republican brinkmanship."

### **4.3 Damage Control from the White House**

Following President Trump's unexpected remarks, the White House was immediately forced into damage control. In particular, Office of Management and Budget Director Russ Vought had to hold a press conference to "clarify" the President's true intentions.¹⁸

According to Vought's explanation, President Trump still believes that, tactically, it is important to include a debt ceiling "increase" in the Republican reconciliation bill. However, from a philosophical and long-term perspective, he believes the debt ceiling system itself should be eliminated in the future because it gives the opposition party excessive negotiating power and becomes a "hostage" that hinders the execution of the administration's key policies.¹⁸ This explanation suggests that President Trump is thinking on two different tracks: "tactical use in the present moment" and "institutional abolition in the future."

This series of events suggests that President Trump's contradictory statements are not just a matter of policy confusion but an intentional political tactic to maximize his options and centrality in the negotiations. By simultaneously supporting the Republican bill and calling for the abolition of its core mechanism (the debt ceiling), he ensures he can declare victory no matter the outcome. If the bill passes, it is a success for his "big, beautiful bill." If the bill fails and a crisis ensues, he can claim he had warned everyone to abolish the "destructive" debt ceiling. This is a classic "heads I win, tails you lose" political hedge, a way to keep the spotlight on himself and prepare an escape route from any failure.

Furthermore, President Trump's intervention exposes a critical weakness in the modern Republican chain of command. The congressional leadership (like Speaker Johnson and Senate Leader Thune) must execute a complex, high-risk legislative strategy while constantly fearing being publicly undercut by the head of their own party. This forces them to fight a two-front war: negotiating with factions within their party and managing the unpredictable messaging from the White House. This dynamic significantly increases the probability that the Republican legislative effort will end in failure.

## **Chapter 5: Scenario Analysis - Pathways to Resolution or Ruin**

The 2025 debt ceiling confrontation has several potential outcomes. This chapter outlines the three most likely scenarios, evaluating the triggers, results, and economic and political impacts of each.

### **5.1 Scenario A: Republican Reconciliation Succeeds (Probability: Low to Medium)**

- **Trigger:** The Republican leadership succeeds in uniting its narrow majorities in both the House and Senate. A compromise is reached on the SALT deduction dispute, and fiscal hawks ultimately vote in favor of the bill. President Trump strongly calls for party unity, suppressing any moves toward defection.
    
- **Result:** The "megabill" is passed and enacted along party lines. The debt ceiling is raised by approximately $4 trillion,²⁶ the TCJA tax cuts are extended, and the planned spending cuts are implemented.
    
- **Impact:**
    
    - **Economic Impact:** A default is averted in the short term, and financial markets are relieved. However, according to CBO analysis, this bill would add trillions to the national deficit over the long term,¹⁵ creating upward pressure on future interest rates.
        
    - **Political Impact:** This would be a major political victory for President Trump and the Republican Party. However, because it is passed through a purely partisan process, it would further deepen America's political divisions. Moreover, according to analysis by the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities (CBPP), a $4 trillion increase would be insufficient, and the debt ceiling would likely be reached again in the fall of 2026, with a new crisis re-emerging in early 2027.²⁶ This is merely a postponement of the problem, not a fundamental solution.
        

### **5.2 Scenario B: Reconciliation Fails, Bipartisan Compromise Follows (Probability: High)**

- **Trigger:** The Republican reconciliation bill fails to secure the necessary votes in the House or Senate due to defections from fiscal hawks or the SALT caucus.³⁰ The X-Date draws near, and panic spreads through the markets.
    
- **Result:** The Republican leadership is forced to negotiate with the Democrats to avoid a default. At the last minute, a bipartisan agreement similar to the 2023 "Fiscal Responsibility Act"³⁵ is formed. The content would likely be a "clean" suspension of the debt ceiling until after the 2026 midterm elections, accompanied by minimal policy changes to allow both parties to save face.
    
- **Impact:**
    
    - **Economic Impact:** A default and economic catastrophe are averted. However, the longer the negotiations drag on, the higher the market uncertainty and the potential for a temporary spike in U.S. Treasury yields.
        
    - **Political Impact:** This would be a significant political defeat for the Republican leadership and President Trump, who failed to deliver on their main promise despite controlling the government. Democrats would score political points for acting as the "responsible party" and holding the upper hand in negotiations. However, the fundamental fiscal imbalances of the U.S. would be left completely unaddressed and postponed.
        

### **5.3 Scenario C: Prolonged Stalemate and Technical Default (Probability: Low, Impact: Catastrophic)**

- **Trigger:** Both Scenarios A and B fail. A combination of political miscalculation, ideological rigidity, and a lack of leadership results in Congress failing to act before the X-Date.
    
- **Result:** The U.S. Treasury becomes unable to meet all its payment obligations on time. The Treasury might attempt to prioritize payments, such as interest on government bonds, but the legal basis and technical feasibility of such a move are highly questionable.⁵
    
- **Impact:**
    
    - **Economic Impact:** Catastrophic. Analyses by firms like Moody's Analytics suggest a default would trigger a severe recession, millions of job losses,⁶ a sharp spike in interest rates, and a permanent downgrade of U.S. debt.
        
    - **Global Impact:** The status of the U.S. dollar as the world's primary reserve currency would be shaken, potentially leading to a global financial crisis.³⁴ This is a "mutually assured destruction" scenario that all rational actors will strive to avoid.

## **Chapter 6: Assessing the "Validity" of a Legislative Outcome**

The core question of this report is to evaluate what kind of legislative solution to the 2025 debt ceiling crisis can be considered "valid." This is not just about whether a bill passes, but about whether its content and the process by which it passes are sound for the future of the United States. This chapter presents an analytical framework to define this "validity" and evaluates each of the scenarios from the previous chapter against these criteria.

### **6.1 Defining "Validity": Three Axes of Evaluation**

To assess whether the passage of a bill is "valid," we use the following three criteria:

1. **Economic Stability:** Does the solution avert a default, prevent financial market turmoil, and maintain the foundation for sustainable economic growth?
    
2. **Fiscal Responsibility:** Does the solution contribute to putting U.S. finances on a more sustainable path? Or does it increase the burden on future generations?
    
3. **Political Governability:** Does the solution demonstrate a functional and repeatable governing process? Or does it deepen political divisions and promote institutional decay?
    

### **6.2 Evaluating Each Scenario**

Based on these three axes, we assess the "validity" of each scenario.

- **Scenario A (Republican Success):** This path clears the immediate test of **Economic Stability** by raising the debt ceiling. However, as CBO analysis shows, it adds trillions of dollars to future debt, meaning it completely fails the test of **Fiscal Responsibility**.¹⁵ Furthermore, by relying on a highly partisan process and worsening political divisions, it is not a sustainable model for addressing future fiscal challenges and is thus highly problematic from the perspective of **Political Governability**.
    
    - **Conclusion: Low Validity.**
        
- **Scenario B (Bipartisan Compromise):** This path is highly rated on the criterion of **Economic Stability** because it avoids a default. However, by intentionally postponing all major fiscal decisions, it receives a very low rating on **Fiscal Responsibility**. Its rating on **Political Governability** is mixed. While it demonstrates that compromise is possible under extreme duress, it also reinforces a vicious cycle of crisis-driven policymaking.
    
    - **Conclusion: Low to Medium Validity.**
        
- **Scenario C (Default):** This path is the epitome of an "invalid" outcome. It represents a catastrophic failure on all criteria: **Economic Stability, Fiscal Responsibility, and Political Governability.**
    
    - **Conclusion: No Validity.**
        

### **6.3 The Flawed Premise**

What emerges from this evaluation is the fact that no realistic legislative outcome can be considered fully "valid," as each fails on at least one critical dimension. The Republican plan is fiscally unsustainable, and the most likely bipartisan agreement is a fiscally hollow act of crisis aversion.

This analysis reveals that the problem lies not with any specific piece of legislation, but with the debt ceiling institution itself. As a governing mechanism, the debt ceiling has proven ineffective at controlling debt,⁶ while artificially creating systemic economic risk and promoting political dysfunction. In this respect, the fact that two politicians from opposite ends of the ideological spectrum—President Trump and Senator Warren—found a strange consensus on abolishing the debt ceiling is highly revealing.¹⁸ It shows a growing recognition across the ideological divide that the tool itself is broken.

Therefore, the most "valid" long-term course of action would be to abolish the debt ceiling system and return the difficult but necessary debate over fiscal policy to the regular annual budget and appropriations process.

### **7.2 Recommendations for International Stakeholders**

#### **Financial and Investment Strategy**

- Proactively consider hedging strategies against a sharp rise in market volatility in the third quarter of 2025.
    
- Review exposure to U.S. T-Bills maturing around the projected X-Date (July-September) and consider reducing it to mitigate the risk of payment delays.
    
- In the event of a default (Scenario C), expect a flight to quality to other safe-haven assets like German Bunds and Japanese Government Bonds, as well as gold, and a sharp global stock market sell-off. Portfolios should be reconfigured to prepare for this possibility.
    

#### **Corporate and Business Strategy**

- Business plans for 2025-2026 should incorporate the possibility of a U.S. economic recession and rising borrowing costs as a risk scenario.
    
- The political outcome of the negotiations must be closely watched. A Republican success (Scenario A) would bring significant changes to U.S. tax and regulatory policy, while a bipartisan agreement (Scenario B) would mean continued policy uncertainty.
    

#### **Geopolitical Assessment**

- It is essential to recognize that repeated debt ceiling crises, even when ultimately resolved, will surely erode U.S. credibility and global leadership.³⁷ This was explicitly cited as a reason for the U.S. credit rating downgrades by agencies like Fitch and Moody's.⁵
    
- A default would accelerate the search for alternatives to the U.S. dollar as the world's primary reserve currency.³⁴ Japanese stakeholders should, from a long-term perspective, begin planning for a transition to a more multipolar global financial system. The debt ceiling issue is not just a domestic U.S. problem; it is a critical juncture that will shape the future of the global financial order.
