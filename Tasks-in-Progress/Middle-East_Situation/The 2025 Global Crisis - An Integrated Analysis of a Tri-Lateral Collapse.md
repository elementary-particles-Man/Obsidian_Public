## **Part III: Internal Collapse of a Superpower - U.S. Governmental Dysfunction**

The escalation of the June 2025 global crisis to an uncontrollable level was underpinned by the severe internal collapse of the United States, the nation that should have been the sole superpower. ### **3.1. The Debt Ceiling as a Weapon of Self-Destruction**

The debt ceiling is a law that sets a cap on the total amount of money the U.S. Treasury can borrow to meet its existing legal obligations. However, this system, unique to the U.S. and Denmark,¹² has in recent years functioned not as a tool for fiscal discipline but as an instrument of partisan political warfare.³⁹

**The Timeline of Paralysis in 2025**

The 2025 crisis occurred at the worst possible time, with this self-destructive mechanism perfectly synchronized with the international crisis.

- **January 2:** The suspension of the debt ceiling under the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 expired. The limit was reset at approximately $36.1 trillion.⁴²
    
- **January 21:** The Treasury Department announced it had begun implementing "extraordinary measures"—accounting maneuvers to avoid a sovereign default.⁴⁶
    
- **March-May:** Multiple agencies, including the Congressional Budget Office (CBO) and the Bipartisan Policy Center (BPC), projected that the "X-Date"—the day when extraordinary measures would be exhausted—would arrive as early as June, or by August or September at the latest.⁴⁷ This forecast perfectly coincided with the timing of the Israeli attack on Iran.
    

During this period, Washington's political machinery ground to a complete halt. The Republican-controlled Congress demanded large-scale spending cuts (up to $2.5 trillion) as a condition for raising the debt ceiling.⁴² Meanwhile, the Democratic Party insisted on a "clean" increase without preconditions, as had been done many times under past administrations.⁴⁶

Adding to the chaos were President Trump's own contradictory statements. He publicly called for the "complete elimination" of the debt ceiling itself on his social media platform, echoing the position of liberal Senator Elizabeth Warren.⁵⁶ Yet, at the same time, his administration was attempting to use the partisan process of budget reconciliation to pass a massive bill that included a $4 trillion increase in the limit.⁵⁶ This incoherent leadership made negotiations even more difficult and severely damaged both domestic and international confidence in America's ability to govern.

**Economic Consequences: Downgrades and Rising Interest Rates**

This political brinkmanship inflicted real economic damage. Following downgrades by S&P in 2011 and Fitch in 2023, Moody's finally stripped the U.S. of its top-tier Aaa credit rating on May 16, 2025.⁴³ The rating agencies consistently cited not only the rising debt itself but also the "erosion of governance" and the "decline in governability due to political polarization."⁴³

The downgrade and default fears pushed up yields on U.S. Treasury bonds, increasing the nation's borrowing costs.⁴⁰ Ironically, the political struggle to reduce the fiscal deficit ended up exacerbating future deficits by increasing interest payments. A default would have had catastrophic consequences, including a severe recession, millions of job losses, and the potential collapse of the global financial system.⁴³

### **3.2. Systemic Overload: A Nation Incapable of Responding to Multiple Crises**

This report defines the state of the United States in 2025 as one of "systemic overload." This refers to a condition where a nation's political and administrative systems, when faced simultaneously with multiple crises each demanding immense resources and leadership focus, become saturated and fall into a state of dysfunction.

In 2025, the U.S. was in precisely this state.

1. **A Hot War in the Middle East:** The Israel-Iran war and the resulting nuclear escalation crisis between India and Pakistan.

3. **A Cold War in Asia:** The intensifying strategic competition with China.
    
4. **A Governance Collapse at Home:** The full-blown political civil war symbolized by the debt ceiling crisis.
    

Each of these crises would, on its own, merit the full attention of the White House National Security Council (NSC) and the President himself. However, in a state of systemic overload, the allocation of resources—financial, military, diplomatic, and leadership attention—becomes impossible. Responding to one crisis impedes the response to others, and the ability to formulate and execute a coherent national strategy is lost.

In this context, the debt ceiling crisis was no longer just a domestic fiscal issue; it transformed into a symptom of America's geopolitical impotence. Historically, debates over the debt limit were, at least ostensibly, about fiscal discipline.⁴⁴ However, as data shows, these political battles have failed to curb the growth of debt, with its trajectory consistently upward.⁴⁵

The 2025 crisis demonstrated a fundamental change in this mechanism. The fight was no longer about spending but about the ideological struggle between the two major parties and a zero-sum game for power. And this internal conflict consumed the political energy and national unity essential for confronting external threats at the very moment they were most needed.

## In conclusion, the domestic political institution of the debt ceiling became the most potent asymmetric weapon for America's adversaries. The Israeli attack on Iran placed the Gulf monarchies in an extremely difficult position. On one hand, they needed to officially condemn Israel's "aggression" to placate their own populations and the broader sentiment of the Islamic world. Saudi Arabia, in particular, as the "Custodian of the Two Holy Mosques," could not ignore the call for Islamic solidarity. On the other hand, the attack was a strategic godsend for them. For years, the greatest security threat to the Gulf states had been an Iran that was developing nuclear weapons and pursuing regional hegemony. If Israel could significantly degrade Iran's nuclear and military capabilities without them having to lift a finger, there could be no greater benefit. The attack would achieve their ultimate goal of containing Iran, a goal they could not accomplish themselves.

From this contradictory situation, their "double game" was born. Publicly, they condemned Israel and called for a diplomatic solution. But behind the scenes, it is highly likely they hoped for the success of the Israeli operation and may have provided limited, unofficial support (such as intelligence sharing or tacit approval of airspace passage). Their top priority was not religious solidarity or the Palestinian cause, but the removal of the Iranian threat and the secure survival of their own monarchies.

This pragmatic calculation is further reinforced by the ambitious economic and social transformations that Saudi Arabia and the UAE have placed at the core of their national strategies. Saudi Arabia's "Vision 2030" and the UAE's "Centennial 2071" both aim to transition away from oil-dependent economies toward technology-driven, knowledge-based ones.⁷⁷ And the engine of this transformation is Artificial Intelligence (AI).

To achieve this national goal, both countries have been making unprecedented investments to secure access to the cutting-edge U.S. AI technology ecosystem. This is not mere portfolio investment; it is a strategic acquisition on which the future of their nations depends.

- **Saudi Arabia:** Through HUMAIN, a new company under its sovereign wealth fund, has partnered with NVIDIA to build "AI Factories" equipped with hundreds of thousands of the latest GPUs.¹⁴ As an initial step, it has ordered 18,000 of NVIDIA's state-of-the-art GB300 chips and plans to deploy 500 megawatts of AMD systems and 500 megawatts of NVIDIA systems over the next five years.¹⁵ Furthermore, it has secured a $5 billion contract with AWS and large-scale investments from other U.S. tech giants like Oracle and Google.¹⁵

The new bargain they have found is "capital for technology." By investing vast oil wealth, they are importing the most important strategic asset of the 21st century—AI technology—from the United States. By deeply embedding themselves in the technological core of America—NVIDIA, Microsoft, Google, Oracle—they are building a new form of security that is far more robust and enduring than any military alliance. Technological interdependence has become an "insurance policy" more valuable than any defense treaty. As long as this strategic goal remains, it is unthinkable that they would get deeply involved in an ideological conflict like a "Three-Religion War" and jeopardize their relationship with the United States.

The technology-driven modernization being pursued by the Gulf states has both a light and a dark side. In parallel with economic development, they are actively adopting state-of-the-art surveillance technologies to manage and control their populations.

Ironically, one of the main suppliers of this technology is Israel, a country they are publicly hostile to. Here, the fundamental contradiction of Gulf modernization is exposed. On one hand, they are trying to build open, innovative knowledge economies to attract global talent and capital.⁷⁷ But at the same time, to maintain their absolute monarchies, they are constructing closed AI surveillance states that stifle all dissent.⁶⁷ What they want are the economic and technological benefits of a globalized network society, but not the political and social freedoms that usually accompany it. This "double game" is no longer confined to the realm of foreign policy; it has become the core of their domestic governance model.

## **Part V: The Final Battlefield - Internal Ideological Struggle and Conclusion**

Integrating all previous analyses, the June 2025 crisis marks a definitive structural shift in the dynamics of international politics. While the "ceasefire" brokered by President Trump temporarily froze direct military conflict between states, it does not signify the end of the crisis. Rather, it indicates that the main battlefield has shifted from _between states_ to _within states_. This chapter will outline this new conflict structure, analyze the internal fractures facing Israel and the Gulf States, and present the ultimate question that will determine the outcome of this global crisis.

### **5.1. The New Battlefield: Regime Pragmatism vs. Popular Religious Passion**

In the Middle East, the epicenter of the crisis, particularly in the Gulf monarchies, a fierce tug-of-war between two opposing forces is now underway.

On one side is **"regime pragmatism."** This is the pragmatic, globally-oriented national strategy pursued by the leaders of Saudi Arabia and the UAE. Their top priority is not ideology or religious solidarity, but the perpetuation of their monarchies and economic prosperity. As a means to this end, they tacitly welcome Israel's military action that degrades the Iranian threat, while simultaneously investing vast capital in the U.S. AI ecosystem as the source of future wealth.¹⁴ Their worldview is defined by the cross-border flows of capital and technology.

On the other side is **"popular religious passion."** This is the sense of pan-Islamic solidarity and humiliation stirred up by the Israeli attack on Iran and the ongoing issue of Jerusalem's holy sites. This sentiment serves as a powerful mobilizing force, especially for those dissatisfied with the regime and for existing Islamist movements like the Muslim Brotherhood.⁷⁴ Their worldview is defined by religious identity and a righteous indignation against the desecration of "holy land" by non-believers.

The clash between these two forces will be the new main battlefield. The regimes will attempt to manage and suppress this religious passion using AI-driven surveillance systems and the distribution of economic benefits.⁶⁷ However, among the populace, resentment will fester over the perception that their leaders are dealing with the "enemies of Islam" and betraying the religious cause. This dynamic is further complicated by the forces of globalization as described by sociologists like Manuel Castells and Arjun Appadurai.¹⁰⁰ The Gulf rulers actively embrace the global "finanscapes" (flows of capital) and "technoscapes" (flows of technology), but they fear and seek to suppress the "ideoscapes" (flows of ideas) that inevitably accompany them—ideas like democracy, human rights, and transnational religious solidarity that threaten their authority. This contradiction is the greatest vulnerability of the Gulf states in the 21st century.

### **5.2. Israel's Internal Struggle: Regime Maintenance vs. Religious Escalation**

This pattern of internal conflict is not limited to the Gulf states. Israel, the supposed "victor," also faces a crisis of deep internal division. Even before the 2025 crisis, Israeli society was severely polarized between a secular, liberal, internationalist "tribe" and a religious, nationalistic, messianic "tribe."¹⁰⁵

The symbol of this conflict was the controversial judicial overhaul. This was an attempt by the religious-right coalition led by Netanyahu to establish political and ideological dominance over the state institutions, such as the judiciary, which they perceived as being controlled by the secular establishment.¹⁰⁶

The military "victory" over Iran is more likely to intensify this domestic conflict than to quell it. This victory, framed by a messianic narrative ("Operation Rising Lion"), will undoubtedly bolster the authority of Prime Minister Netanyahu and his supporting religious-right bloc. They will see this victory as proof of the righteousness of their worldview and will likely push their domestic agenda—judicial reform, Haredi (ultra-Orthodox) military draft exemptions, and West Bank settlement expansion—even more forcefully.

This will provoke strong backlash from the secular and liberal segments of society, further deepening the fissures in Israeli society. The result of a "victory" in an existential foreign war is not national unity, but a more severe internal conflict over the fundamental character of the Jewish state. The paradox of a strategic victory against an external enemy intensifying a domestic "culture war" and causing societal collapse from within is Israel's "victor's dilemma."

### **5.3. Conclusion - The AI Gamble: Can Prosperity Override Identity?**

Integrating all analyses, the 2025 global crisis presents us with the most fundamental and unresolved strategic question. It is the grand experiment upon which the Gulf monarchs are betting their nations' futures: **"Can the economic prosperity and convenience brought by AI override the powerful forces of religious and ethnic identity and humiliation?"**

The Gulf monarchies are making a historic wager. They believe that by building AI-powered "smart cities" and providing their citizens with comfortable, convenient lives and economic wealth, they can pacify the religious and tribal loyalties, as well as the collective Islamic humiliation stirred by the attack on Iran, and thus maintain their regimes.¹⁰⁸ Fully automated services, personalized consumer experiences, and security guaranteed by surveillance—can these things overcome the demands of ancient identities?

Several historical cases offer insight into this question.

- **The "Asian Values" Debate:** The model advocated by figures like Singapore's former Prime Minister Lee Kuan Yew, which prioritizes economic development and social order over Western-style individual freedoms, achieved a degree of success.¹¹¹ The Gulf states are attempting to realize a 21st-century version of this model using the powerful tool of AI.
    
- **Economic Development and Ethnic Nationalism:** However, counterexamples are also numerous. In the Balkans, the collapse of the economy and the failure of promised prosperity ultimately led to the resurgence of destructive ethnic nationalism.¹¹⁷ And modern China, while achieving phenomenal economic growth, has simultaneously pursued a policy of thorough cultural and religious repression against the Uyghurs.¹¹⁸ These cases suggest that economic benefits alone are often insufficient to completely erase the demands of powerful ethnic and religious identities.
    

The Gulf states are constructing a new social contract, a fusion of consumerism and surveillance. But globalization and information technology, while bringing economic prosperity, also provide the tools for oppressed identities to resonate and connect across borders. What will happen when popular religious passion rejects the "smart life" offered by the regime as an opiate?

## The success or failure of this "AI gamble" is the greatest uncertainty that will determine the stability of the Middle East, and by extension the world, after 2025. The answer to it is also an answer to a question of human history: whether technology can ultimately satisfy humanity's most fundamental desires for identity and dignity.
