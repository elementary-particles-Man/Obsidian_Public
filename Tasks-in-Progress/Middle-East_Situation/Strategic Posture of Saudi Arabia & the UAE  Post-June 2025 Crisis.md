# **Analysis of Saudi Arabian and UAE Strategic Dynamics Post-June 2025**

**Background:** In the aftermath of the Israeli strike on Iranian nuclear facilities on June 13, 2025 (the so-called "12-Day War"), Saudi Arabia and the UAE have been forced into a delicate balancing act between managing backlash from the Islamic world and maintaining cooperation with the United States and Israel. This analysis outlines their recent responses from three perspectives: religious, economic, and diplomatic.

## **1. Religious Perspective: Islamic World's Reaction and the Gulf States' Response**

- **Advocacy for the Palestinian Cause:** Both governments have traditionally supported the Palestinian cause and maintained a critical stance toward Israel. In October of the previous year, the Saudi Foreign Minister condemned an Israeli military operation in northern Gaza as "genocide" and affirmed that normalization with Israel would not be possible without the establishment of a Palestinian state. In the current conflict with Iran, the official Saudi Press Agency (SPA) strongly condemned the U.S.-backed Israeli attack as a "violation of Iranian sovereignty" and called for de-escalation and a political solution. The UAE, in contrast, avoided explicit condemnation, merely expressing "deep concern" and advocating for a diplomatic resolution.
    
- **Internal Gulf Perspectives:** According to reports from U.S. congressional sources, while Gulf leaders publicly criticized the Israeli attack, they privately expressed a nuanced understanding of the strike against Iran's nuclear capabilities. One source described their position as "not mourning Israel's success, but not celebrating it either," indicating an appreciation for the move to counter the Iranian nuclear threat. Another report suggested they were "awed by Israel's actions and relieved that the Iranian threat was being addressed." This reveals a dual-track approach: maintaining a public façade of criticism while privately prioritizing the neutralization of the Iranian nuclear issue.
    
- **Religious and Public Opinion Trends:** Both Saudi Arabia and the UAE have deeply conservative Islamic populations, and there is a tangible risk that reconciliation with Israel could be perceived as a "blasphemy against Islam by Judeo-Christian forces." However, both states maintain strict control over security and public expression, with past instances of activists being detained for criticizing normalization with Israel. Consequently, the governments maintain a moderate public stance. In any case, they have kept their distance from pro-Iranian actors (such as Hezbollah, Turkey, and Pakistan-aligned groups), and a pivot toward an anti-U.S./anti-Israel policy has not occurred.
    
- **Diplomatic Maneuvers:** At the request of Iran, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Oman engaged with the U.S. to press Israel for a ceasefire. This action is interpreted not as a religiously motivated move but as a pragmatic diplomatic effort aimed at preventing regional escalation and promoting a political resolution.
    

## **2. Balancing with Economic and AI Strategy**

- **The Drive for AI Supremacy:** Both nations have staked their national futures on economic diversification and technological leadership, with massive investments in the field of Artificial Intelligence. In May 2025, during a visit by President Trump, Saudi Arabia announced major deals with U.S. companies (including Nvidia and AMD). Through its new entity "Humain," the kingdom plans to acquire hundreds of thousands of state-of-the-art AI chips. The Saudi Public Investment Fund (PIF) also signed MOUs to invest tens of billions of dollars in U.S.-based AI firms. Similarly, the UAE is advancing plans with Amazon (AWS) for AI data centers and is expected to receive large volumes of Nvidia chips by the end of the year.
    
- **Prioritizing Pragmatic Interests:** This massive investment is driven by a logic that prioritizes tangible benefits. The Saudi Foreign Minister has explicitly stated that "trade and AI cooperation between Saudi Arabia and the U.S. is not linked to a third country (Israel) and can probably progress quite quickly," signaling a clear policy of decoupling economic cooperation from the politically sensitive issue of normalization with Israel. In essence, both nations are banking on the economic development and security advantages derived from AI technology to manage and contain domestic and international religious backlash.
    

## **3. The Dilemma: Maintaining or Shifting Relations with Israel**

- **Current Status of Israel Relations:** The UAE, a signatory of the 2020 Abraham Accords, has expanding economic and security cooperation with Israel. During the recent Iran attack, it remained publicly silent while privately appreciating the action against the nuclear threat, showing no signs of severing ties. Saudi Arabia, while not having formally normalized relations, has conditioned such a move on "a pathway for the Palestinian people," a stance that allows it to prioritize its security partnership with the U.S. while keeping the door open for future normalization should the Palestinian issue see progress.
    
- **No Pivot to Iran or Anti-U.S. Blocs:** Neither country has shown any indication of moving closer to Iran or anti-U.S. actors like Hezbollah. Despite restoring diplomatic ties with Iran in 2023, the Saudi Foreign Minister noted that the relationship, while positive, "remains difficult." The pragmatic benefits derived from military and intelligence cooperation with the U.S.—including access to advanced weaponry and defense pacts—are considered far more valuable than pursuing ideological "legitimacy" at the cost of these strategic assets.
    
- **The Equilibrium Point:** Ultimately, for both Saudi Arabia and the UAE, the strategic economic benefits of acquiring AI technology outweigh the domestic religious backlash from maintaining a tacit relationship with Israel. Both governments continue to publicly call for ceasefires and diplomatic efforts while simultaneously advancing their economic and security agreements. Therefore, their strategy is not a binary choice between "accepting religious humiliation to gain technology and security" and "choosing Islamic-Arab legitimacy by severing ties." Rather, it is a sophisticated dual-track strategy to manage both. For instance, when the U.S. brokered a ceasefire on June 23, leaders from both nations publicly expressed sympathy for the victims (while remaining cautious about defending Israel) but in practice welcomed the de-escalation and focused on preventing further conflict.
