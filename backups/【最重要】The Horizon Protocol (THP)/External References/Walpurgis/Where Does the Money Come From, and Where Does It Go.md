# Where Does the Money Come From, and Where Does It Go?: A Basic Guide to Government Finance for Understanding Walpurgis

## Introduction: Why This Story Matters

A scenario is anticipated on September 30, 2025, where the global financial system will be hit by a massive storm codenamed "Walpurgis." At the heart of this storm is the collapse of trust in the "money" of the United States.

But what is national money, anyway? How is it different from our household budgets, and how does it move?

This guide explains its most basic mechanics without jargon. Understanding this "story of money" is the first and most crucial step to comprehending the essence of the coming crisis.

> (Note: This guide focuses on understanding the basic concepts of money as a story. For a more technical explanation of the operational flow, please read the companion guide, **"Budget and Execution Mechanics (English Guide)."**)

## Chapter 1: The Government's Wallet Is Nothing Like Yours

Many people tend to think, "The government, like a household, should live within its means—its tax revenue." This is known as the **"household budget fallacy,"** and it is the **fundamental misunderstanding that serves as the source of Walpurgis.**

### Your Wallet vs. The Government's Wallet

|Your Wallet|The Government's Wallet|
|---|---|
|**You have to earn money.**|**It can issue the money itself.**|
|You borrow dollars from a bank.|The U.S. Treasury’s spending is settled through the Federal Reserve system, bringing dollars into existence operationally.|
|If you can't pay your debts, you go bankrupt.|It cannot be forced into insolvency in its own currency (policy and inflation constraints still apply).|

**To use a story as an analogy...**

Your wallet is like a **"bucket" that runs dry if you don't fill it with water.** The money you earn is the water; if you use it, it's gone.

The government's wallet, on the other hand, is the **"faucet"** itself. It can create its own money (water), so in principle, it can never run out.

> (※Note: This is strictly about the _ability to pay_. If the faucet is turned on without limit, it will cause severe inflation. Policy choices always have consequences.)

### So, Why Does the Government "Borrow" by Issuing Bonds?

If the government can create money, why does it bother borrowing through "Treasury bonds"?

The reason is **to regulate the amount of money circulating in the economy.**

If the government were to issue money infinitely (leaving the faucet wide open), the value of that money would plummet, causing hyperinflation.

So, the government first "borrows" money from banks and other investors in the form of bonds. This allows it to control the amount of money it supplies to the economy. Bonds act like the **"gate of a dam,"** regulating the flow.

## Chapter 2: The Secret of the Two Markets

The bonds issued by the government are traded in two different markets. Understanding the difference between these two is the key to understanding what will happen in America.

### Market #1: The Primary Market (The "Premiere")

- **Participants:** Only the government and a select group of very large financial institutions (the **Primary Dealers**, or PDs).
    
- **Purpose:** This is where the government sells its **"brand new"** bonds to the PDs.
    

Think of it as a **"premiere"** for a new blockbuster movie. The studio (the government) only shows it to exclusive distributors (the PDs). The general public can't get in at this stage.

### Market #2: The Secondary Market (The "Public Theater")

- **Participants:** All investors, including us.
    
- **Purpose:** This is where the bonds the PDs have bought are freely bought and sold.
    

This is the **"public movie theater."** We buy our tickets (bonds) from the distributors here, or sell them to others.

## Chapter 3: The Radically Different Rules of the "Theater" in Japan and the U.S.

The rules of this "theater" are completely different in Japan and the U.S. This difference explains why only America faces Walpurgis.

### The Rules of the Japanese "Theater" (Secondary Market)

- **Distributor (PD) Strategy:** The general approach is to **"hold the bonds until maturity."**
    
- **The Theater's "Guardian Angel":** If the price of bonds starts to fall in the theater, a **"guardian angel" called the Bank of Japan (BoJ)** will step in to buy them. This allows the distributors to hold their inventory without fear.
    
- **The Result:** In Japan, the popularity in the theater (bond prices) does not immediately dictate the brand's (the nation's) credibility. Because the guardian angel is always there, the market remains relatively stable.
  
  In short: prices are cushioned; day-to-day yields map only weakly to perceived sovereign credit.
    

### The Rules of the American "Theater" (Secondary Market)

- **Distributor (PD) Strategy:** The core business model is to **"flip the bonds immediately for a profit."** Treasuries are not long-term assets; they are inventory to be moved.
    
- **There Is No** _**Automatic**_ **Guardian Angel:** The U.S. central bank, the Federal Reserve (the Fed), is **not** a guaranteed savior. While the Fed can deploy facilities (e.g., repo operations, the Standing Repo Facility, or QE) at its discretion, there is no standing, guaranteed backstop for dealers in all conditions. Its independence is paramount, and some of its most powerful crisis-fighting tools may require **explicit or implicit Congressional approval.** The Fed's experts understand the plumbing, but the politics are difficult. The very Congress from which authority may be required is often dominated by the **"household budget fallacy."** Politicians, believing the government can "run out of money," may be unwilling to authorize the actions needed to stabilize the system. The distributors are, by design, largely on their own.
    
- **The Result:** In America, popularity in the theater (bond prices) is **directly tied to the brand's (the nation's) credibility.** If the tickets stop selling, it means the brand's value has instantly collapsed.
  
  In short: prices are the signal; yields map directly to perceived sovereign risk across the curve.
    

## Chapter 4: The Nature of Walpurgis — What Happens in the American Theater

With this understanding, the Walpurgis scenario becomes clear.

1. **Trouble at the Premiere (Primary Market):** As trust in the American brand erodes, **even the distributors (PDs) will refuse to buy the new product** at the premiere on September 30. (In practice, this shows up as an auction that only clears with an unusually large tail and low bid-to-cover — a functional failure.)
    
2. **Forced Distribution:** Under primary dealer obligations, PDs can be compelled to absorb residual supply.
    
3. **Panic in the Public Theater (Secondary Market):** The distributors are now stuck with massive amounts of toxic inventory (worthless bonds). With no automatic guardian angel to call, they panic. To survive, they begin a fire sale of **everything they own (not just bonds, but stocks and other assets too).**
    
4. **The Global Financial System Halts:** U.S. Treasury bonds are used as collateral for financial transactions worldwide. The collapse of their value means the foundation of the entire system has crumbled. **Every "theater" across the globe shuts down at once.**
    

This is the nature of the phenomenon called "Narrow Walpurgis."

## Conclusion: Why We Started with a Story About Taxes

Let's return to the beginning. Why is the "household budget fallacy" so dangerous?

Because it makes politicians forget that the government has a **"faucet"** and forces them to operate using only the **"water in the bucket"** collected from taxes.

- **The True Role of Taxes:** Taxes are not the government's "funding." They are the **"drain,"** used to pull excess money out of the economy to prevent inflation. They are the **"thermostat"** for regulating the economic temperature.
    

American politicians have forgotten about the faucet and are trying to run the country on water from the drain. In doing so, they are turning off their own water supply, breaking the dam's gate, and are about to unleash a global flood.

May this story help you understand the crisis to come.

### Mini-Glossary

- **Primary Dealers (PD):** A small group of large financial institutions authorized to buy new government bonds directly from the Treasury. The "distributors."
  
- **Primary Market:** The market where the government sells new bonds to the PDs. The "premiere."
  
- **Secondary Market:** The market where investors trade already-issued bonds. The "public theater."
  
- **Tail:** The difference between the expected yield and the actual yield at an auction. A large tail is a sign of auction stress/dysfunction.
  
- **Bid-to-Cover (B/C):** The ratio of total bids received to the amount offered. A low ratio signals weak demand.
  
- **Failed Auction:** Formal failures are rare. In practice, markets treat auctions that clear only with an unusually large tail and low B/C as functionally failed.
