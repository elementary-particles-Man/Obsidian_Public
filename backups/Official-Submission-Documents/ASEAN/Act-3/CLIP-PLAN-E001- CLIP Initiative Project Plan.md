# Community Livelihood and Infrastructure Project (CLIP)

**Document No.:** CLIP-PLAN-E001 **Classification:** FOR OFFICIAL USE / FOR DEVELOPMENT PARTNERS **Subject:** A Project Plan for Humanitarian Assistance and Economic Reconstruction in Conflict-Affected Border Regions

## 1. Executive Summary

The Community Livelihood and Infrastructure Project (CLIP) is the humanitarian and economic reconstruction counterpart to the TIGA (Transnational Integrated Governance Assistance) Initiative. While TIGA addresses the structural security threats posed by state-function hijack, CLIP focuses on the immediate human consequences of the conflict and lays the groundwork for long-term, sustainable peace.

This document outlines a concrete project plan for international development and financial institutions. CLIP aims to provide immediate humanitarian relief, restore essential infrastructure and livelihoods, and strengthen local governance, thereby building trust with local populations and creating a virtuous cycle of stability and development.

## 2. Strategic Context: The Other Side of the Coin

The TIGA initiative is designed to dismantle the criminal-state nexus. However, lasting stability cannot be achieved through security measures alone. The conflict has severely impacted civilian populations, displacing communities, destroying infrastructure, and disrupting local economies.

CLIP is designed to operate in parallel with TIGA, addressing these critical needs. It serves as a tangible demonstration of the international community's commitment not only to regional security but also to the well-being of its people.

## 3. Core Principles

The CLIP initiative will be guided by the following core principles:

- **Human Security:** Placing the safety and dignity of individuals and communities at the center of all activities.
    
- **Local Ownership:** Working in close partnership with local communities and credible civil society organizations to ensure projects meet genuine needs.
    
- **Transparency and Accountability:** Implementing robust monitoring and evaluation mechanisms to ensure all assistance is delivered effectively and without corruption.
    
- **Conflict Sensitivity:** Designing all interventions to mitigate social tensions and contribute positively to peacebuilding.
    

## 4. Priority Action Areas

CLIP will focus on four interconnected pillars, implemented in a phased approach.

### Pillar 1: Immediate Humanitarian Relief

- **Objective:** To alleviate the suffering of populations displaced or affected by the conflict.
    
- **Key Actions:**
    
    - Provision of food, clean water, shelter, and medical supplies in partnership with the WFP, UNICEF, and ICRC.
        
    - Establishment of safe zones and humanitarian corridors for civilians.
        

### Pillar 2: Critical Infrastructure Rehabilitation

- **Objective:** To restore essential public services and create the conditions for economic recovery.
    
- **Key Actions:**
    
    - Repair of damaged roads, bridges, and irrigation systems.
        
    - Reconstruction of schools and local health clinics.
        
    - Restoration of power and communication lines.
        

### Pillar 3: Livelihood and Economic Restoration

- **Objective:** To help communities rebuild their economic foundations and reduce dependency on aid.
    
- **Key Actions:**
    
    - Distribution of seeds, tools, and agricultural inputs to farming communities.
        
    - Provision of micro-grants and vocational training to support the re-establishment of small businesses.
        
    - "Cash-for-work" programs focused on community infrastructure projects.
        

### Pillar 4: Local Governance Support

- **Objective:** To enhance the capacity and legitimacy of local administrative bodies.
    
- **Key Actions:**
    
    - Technical assistance and training for local officials in public financial management and service delivery.
        
    - Support for community-led development planning and budgeting processes.
        
    - Establishment of community-based conflict resolution mechanisms.
        

## 5. Implementation and Funding

- **Coordination:** CLIP will be managed by a consortium of implementing partners, led by institutions such as JICA, the Asian Development Bank (ADB), and the UNDP, under the strategic oversight of the proposed ASEAN Strategic Reconstruction Office.
    
- **Funding:** We propose the establishment of a multi-donor trust fund, managed by a reputable international financial institution like the World Bank, to pool resources and ensure coordinated financial support. Japan is prepared to make a significant initial contribution to this fund. An international donor conference will be convened to secure further pledges.
    

## 6. Conclusion

CLIP is an essential investment in peace. By addressing the root causes of grievance and despair at the community level, it complements the top-down security approach of TIGA. Together, they form a comprehensive strategy to not only end the current crisis but to build a more resilient, prosperous, and stable future for the region.
