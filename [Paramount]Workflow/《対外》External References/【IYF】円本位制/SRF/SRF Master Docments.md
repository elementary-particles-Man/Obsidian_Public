# **Strategic Reconstruction Framework (SRF)**

### _A Global Response to China’s Industrial Collapse and Semiconductor Supply Risks_

**Draft for Policy Planning — Government of Japan / G7 Use**

---

## **I. Executive Summary**

The collapse of China’s manufacturing capacity represents the most significant supply-chain shock since World War II.  
Power shortages, halted chemical production, PCB disruptions, and imminent failure of DRAM/NAND production create a global systemic risk.

This document proposes:

1. **SRF – a G7-led Semiconductor Reconstruction Fund**, centered on Japan and the United States, to substitute China-dependent production with a diversified architecture.
    
2. **THP-0 China – a non-intervention recovery plan**, focused on humanitarian stabilization and city-level governance restoration after CCP political failure.
    

Both operate under _existing international law_, require _no regime-change operations_, and emphasize _transparency_ via the CLEAR verification layer.

---

## **II. Strategic Rationale**

### 1. China’s industrial system is entering irreversible failure

Evidence from power-grid blackouts, water shortages, halted epoxy/chemical production, and worker displacement indicates:

- **No 12–36 month recovery path**
    
- **Permanent loss of semiconductor ecosystem**
    
- **High probability of regional administrative collapse**
    

### 2. Global industries cannot absorb the shock without coordinated replacement

Critical “non-substitutable” sectors:

- DRAM / NAND (Hynix, Samsung)
    
- High-density PCBs
    
- MLCC
    
- Battery components (electrolytes, separators)
    
- Industrial chemicals (epoxy, resins)
    

Japan, Taiwan, U.S., and ASEAN are the only viable substitutes.

## **III. SRF – Semiconductor Reconstruction Fund**

### **1. Purpose**

To rebuild the global semiconductor backbone by replacing China-origin production through G7–ASEAN cooperation.

### **2. Structure**

- **Lead nations**: Japan, U.S.
    
- **Supporting**: EU, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, Vietnam
    
- **Funding**: ¥10 trillion (multi-year issuance), with CLEAR audit layer
    
- **Instruments**:
    
    - JPYC-backed liquidity facilities
        
    - Emergency credit lines
        
    - Relocation subsidies
        
    - Capital expenditure acceleration
        

### **3. Manufacturer-by-Manufacturer Strategy**

#### **Hynix** – _Primary rescue target_

- Technologically competitive
    
- Structurally viable
    
- Requires liquidity, not political protection  
    → **Full SRF support**
    

#### **Samsung** – _Selective support_

- Excessive non-semiconductor exposure
    
- Global overextension  
    → Support restricted to **semiconductor-only lines**
    

#### **Kioxia–Micron Anchor Alliance**

- Redundancy for global DRAM/NAND
    
- Japan + U.S. core  
    → Serve as “dual-capacity backbone”
    

#### **Taiwan ecosystem**

- Distribute risk
    
- Ensure PCB/MLCC continuity  
    → Leverage Foxconn relocation away from China
    

### **4. Sectoral Relocation**

#### **Chemicals (Japan)**

- Epoxy, resins, industrial precursors
    
- Most efficient relocation due to safety, regulation, electricity stability
    

#### **PCBs (ASEAN)**

- Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam
    
- Rapid absorption capacity
    

#### **Batteries (Japan + U.S.)**

- Japan for materials
    
- U.S. for scale
    
- Korea for specialized lines

## **IV. THP-0 China — Post-CCP Reconstruction Plan**

_Humanitarian-first, Non-Intervention, City-Based Model_

### **1. Non-Intervention Principles**

- No military entry
    
- No political engineering
    
- No regime-change operations
    
- Respect for sovereignty
    
- Transparent humanitarian access
    

### **2. Why the City-Based Model?**

China cannot be reconstructed as a single administrative unit due to:

- Infrastructure failures
    
- Province-level divergence
    
- Breakdown of fiscal transfers
    
- Inconsistent governance capacity
    

Therefore, reconstruction uses **City Administrative Zones (CAZ)**:

- Shanghai
    
- Shenzhen
    
- Guangzhou
    
- Chengdu
    
- Chongqing
    
- Tianjin
    
- And others
    

### **3. Operational Layers**

#### **Layer 1: Humanitarian Stability**

- Food, water, medical, evacuation corridors
    
- CLEAR-based verification to avoid corruption
    

#### **Layer 2: Civil Administration Restart**

- Local public services
    
- Policing and safety
    
- Electricity, water, waste systems
    

#### **Layer 3: Economic Restart**

- Industrial zones
    
- SME revival
    
- International logistics resumption
    

Japan contributes:

- Logistics
    
- Verification
    
- Financial stabilizers
    
- No troop deployment
