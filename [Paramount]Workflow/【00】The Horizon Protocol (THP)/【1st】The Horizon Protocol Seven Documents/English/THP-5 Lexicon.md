# THP-5: Lexicon (Unified Version)

This lexicon describes the key concepts in The Horizon Protocol (THP) with a two-tiered structure: **Academic Version (Strict Definition)** and **Explanatory Version (Plain Explanation)**.
Each term explicitly indicates its reference to **THP-2 Ethics Charter article numbers** and related charters/annexes.

---

## Human Morality / Ethics (人倫) [Refer to: THP-2 Preamble, Article 1]

-   **Academic Version**
    "Human morality" is a protective wall necessary for humans to exist as "human beings," and it is the foundation of collective self-defense against the harsh, survival-of-the-fittest environment of reality. Order is maintained by using this human morality as the sole criterion and by categorizing sins into atonement and condemnation.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Human morality is "the path humans should follow." It's like a wall that protects everyone from a harsh world. Acts that break this wall become "sins." If you atone, you can return inside (atonement); if you are deemed unforgivable, you are expelled (condemnation).

---

## Order (秩序) [Refer to: THP-2 Preamble]

-   **Academic Version**
    Order refers to a system that maintains collective self-defense by using human morality as the sole criterion and through atonement and condemnation. The internal norms of individual groups are left to the discretion of that entity, but at the human scale, a universal order based on human morality applies.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Order is "a system for everyone to survive by following the rules of human morality." Each country and organization can have its own rules, but for humanity as a whole, "human morality" becomes the common rule.

---

## Human Rights (人権) [Refer to: THP-2 Article 0]

-   **Academic Version**
    Human rights are not inherent but are granted to entities that uphold human morality through a two-tiered process: (1) the consent of their affiliated group, and (2) the consent of all humanity. Acts that deviate from human morality deny human rights and are placed outside of collective self-defense.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Human rights are not "innate" but "given by everyone to those who uphold human morality." Human rights exist only when the country or society to which that person belongs first recognizes them, and all humanity agrees to it.

---

## Universal Human Rights (普遍的人権) [Refer to: THP-2 Article 0 / THP-3 List of Prohibited Practices]

-   **Academic Version**
    Universal human rights are the minimum standards that take precedence over any culture or religion. Acts that violate the "List of Prohibited Practices" stipulated in THP-3 People's Charter are not permitted, even with cultural or religious justification.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Universal human rights are "the minimum rules that must be absolutely observed by all cultures and religions worldwide." Even ethnic or religious customs cannot infringe upon human rights.

---

## Universal Crimes (普遍的大罪) [Refer to: THP-2 Preamble, Article 2]

-   **Academic Version**
    Universal crimes are acts subject to condemnation by all humanity, limited to three types: large-scale civilian killings, institutional persecution, and repeated aggression. Political issues are excluded.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Universal crimes are "absolutely unforgivable grave sins." Examples are killing large numbers of civilians, persecuting people institutionally, and repeatedly invading. Only these three.

---

## Atonement / Reintegration (贖罪) [Refer to: THP-2 Article 3]

-   **Academic Version**
    Atonement is the official process by which a perpetrator, through fulfilling their responsibilities and pledging to prevent recurrence, returns within the "protective wall of human morality."

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Atonement is "saying sorry, promising not to do it again, and returning to the community."

---

## Condemnation / Irreversible Mark (断罪) [Refer to: THP-2 Article 4]

-   **Academic Version**
    Condemnation is an irreversible historical stigma imposed without recourse on an entity that has committed a universal crime. Its concept overlaps with war crimes, crimes against humanity, and crimes of aggression targeted by existing international law (e.g., Geneva Conventions, Rome Statute [International Criminal Court]). However, condemnation in THP is unique in that it is positioned not merely as a judicial judgment but as an "ethical declaration in the name of humanity."

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Condemnation is "being eternally etched in history as an absolutely unforgivable sin." For example, it is similar to "war crimes" and "crimes against humanity" judged by international law such as the Geneva Conventions and the Rome Statute, but in THP, there is a difference in that it is "declared in the name of all humanity."

---

## Councils of Ethics (人倫評議会) [Refer to: THP-2 Ethical Institutions]

-   **Academic Version**
    The Council of Ethics has a three-tiered structure: (1) Global Council of Ethics (final declaration authority), (2) National Councils of Ethics (investigation/sentencing), (3) Humanity Study Group (advisory reconsideration/request for pardon).

-   **Explanatory Version**
    The Council of Ethics is "three teams." The global top (Global Council of Ethics), national investigators (National Councils of Ethics), and advisors who can plead for leniency (Humanity Study Group).

---

## Humanity Study Group (人倫検討団) [Refer to: THP-2 Special Notes]

-   **Academic Version**
    The Humanity Study Group provisionally consists of the Four Sages and is an advisory body with only the right to request pardon and the right to reconsider condemnation. There is no hierarchy, and it is premised on democratic reconstitution in the future.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    The Humanity Study Group is "representatives of humanity's conscience." Currently, it consists of four provisional members who can only say "please forgive them" or "please reconsider." They do not have great authority and are scheduled to be properly re-elected later.

---

## E-MAD (Economic Mutually Assured Deterrence) [Refer to: THP-2 Article 5 / THP-2-S0]

-   **Academic Version**
    E-MAD is a mechanism that automatically enforces sanctions against entities violating the Ethics Charter through economic means. The details of the MAD score calculation formula are entrusted to THP-2-S0, and implementation using Node.js is underway.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    E-MAD is "a system where economic penalties are automatically imposed if rules are broken." Detailed calculation methods are still under development in a separate file.

---

## Ops-KPI Dashboard [Refer to: THP-7]

-   **Academic Version**
    The monitoring and measurement platform for THP operations. In addition to the regular dashboard for Gate-A to C, there is also a "back dashboard" for Gate-D and beyond, assuming an Aftermath transition.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    The Ops-KPI Dashboard is "a screen to check if THP is working properly." There's a regular version (Gate-A to C) and a secret version for emergencies (Gate-D and beyond).

#### **Walpurgis (ワルプルギス) [Refer to: THP Overall / Successor Concept: ECX (Echo-Collapse Exchange)]**

-   **Academic Version**
    Walpurgis does not refer to a specific single event, but is a general term for a **complex collapse phenomenon (polycrisis)** where structural vulnerabilities latent in multiple layers such as society, economy, technology, and ethics trigger a chain reaction of self-destruction **(cascade collapse)** through mutual resonance (echo). Its ignition point can exist in either internal factors such as governance dysfunction or market structural distortions in the United States, or external factors such as geopolitical chain failures or political rhetoric that shakes trust. In the THP system, this is positioned as a symbolic concept of structural collapse, and in subsequent analysis and practice, it is generalized and universalized as **ECX (Echo-Collapse Exchange)**. That is, Walpurgis is the prototype of ECX, and is the initial definition of a structure where **"safety devices self-destruct and collapse chains."**

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Walpurgis does not mean "the world broke on a certain day." It is the name for a phenomenon where many weaknesses in society and the economy collapse while influencing each other. It was a word that represented a structure of "the flip side of safety" where mechanisms meant to protect destroy themselves. This idea was later organized and developed into a common framework called **ECX (Echo-Collapse Exchange)** in THP. In other words, Walpurgis is the "starting point" where THP first recognized the structure of world collapse, and ECX is the "practical system" for observing, classifying, and controlling that phenomenon. THP is designed as a framework to record this irreversible process of collapse and prevent human society from repeating the same mistakes.

---
#### **ECX (Echo-Collapse Exchange) [Refer to: THP Overall / Former Name: Walpurgis]**

-   **Academic Version**
    ECX is a generalization and redefinition of the structural collapse phenomenon formerly known as "Walpurgis." It refers to the process where safety devices, restraint structures, and adjustment functions self-reverse through mutual resonance (echo), and collapse (collapse) is chained, exchanged, and propagated (exchange). It is an **"echo-collapse exchange phenomenon"** that can occur in all layers such as finance, technology, information, ethics, politics, and ethnicity, and in the THP system, it is observed and analyzed by categorizing it into **ECX-F (financial layer) / ECX-T (technology layer) / ECX-M (information layer) / ECX-E (ethical layer) / ECX-P (political layer) / ECX-N (ethnic layer)**, etc. ECX means that "the health of the system collapses due to its own control signals," and THP is designed as a framework to deter, isolate, and reconstruct this phenomenon.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    ECX (Echo-Collapse Exchange) is the new name for the phenomenon formerly called "Walpurgis," where "the world breaks down by its own mechanisms." For example, when mechanisms meant to protect (laws, economy, technology, justice, etc.) are destroyed by their own echoes, it is called ECX. It happens not only in money and politics but also in information, culture, and people's minds. In THP, this is recorded by categorizing "in which layer and in what form it is occurring" and is monitored and controlled to prevent it from happening again.

## Social Narrative (社会ナラティブ) [Refer to: Ops-KPI Auxiliary Indicator]

-   **Academic Version**
    Social narrative refers to the influence of popular stories and political slogans on the perception of order. It is monitored as an auxiliary indicator for Ops-KPI.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Social narrative is "stories and slogans that everyone believes in." We check this because it moves society.

---

## Household Finance Narrative (家計簿財政) [Refer to: Ops-KPI Auxiliary Indicator]

-   **Academic Version**
    Household finance narrative is a fallacious discourse that equates national finance with a household budget. It is classified as an Ops-KPI auxiliary indicator because it can trigger market panic and irrational political judgments.

-   **Explanatory Version**
    Household finance narrative is "the misunderstanding that national money is the same as a household budget." This can cause confusion in politics and markets, so caution is needed.
