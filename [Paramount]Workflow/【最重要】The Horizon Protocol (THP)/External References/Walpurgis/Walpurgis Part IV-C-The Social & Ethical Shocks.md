# Walpurgis Part IV-C: The Social & Ethical Shocks

## Executive Summary: The Collapse of the Social Contract

This report analyzes the chain of systemic social and ethical shocks triggered by the Dollar Default Crisis (DDC). This analysis assumes the quantitative frameworks established in the preceding reports on financial, political, and resource shocks.

The DDC is not merely a financial crisis; it signifies a rapid and structural collapse of state capacity in developed nations. Its primary transmission channel is the simultaneous destruction of private wealth and state revenue, which drives a self-reinforcing cycle of austerity, mass unemployment, and social unrest. Existing institutional frameworks are fatally unprepared for this type of complex shock.

**Key Projections (Medium-Term Scenario):** In our baseline "L2: Catastrophic Scenario," the following outcomes are projected. Global financial asset losses will reach between **$12 trillion and $21.6 trillion**, severely impacting the real economy. The global U6-equivalent unemployment rate will rise by **10 to 15 percentage points** from the baseline, a scale comparable to the Great Depression. This will trigger international and internal displacement (migration and refugees) of **50 to 80 million people** within the first six months. Violent and property crimes will increase by **30% to 50%** year-over-year in developed countries, leading to a significant deterioration of social order.

**Key Regional Vulnerabilities:** The impact of the shock will vary significantly by region.

- **North America & Europe:** As the epicenters of the financial system, they will face the most severe challenges from labor market collapse and the erosion of social cohesion. The EU will be threatened by a governance dilemma between fiscal discipline (the Stability and Growth Pact) and responding to social unrest.
    
- **MENA & Sub-Saharan Africa:** The loss of ability to import food and energy will lead to widespread famine and state failure, creating unprecedented migratory pressure towards Europe.
    
- **East Asia:** The evaporation of export demand, leading to cascading bankruptcies in the manufacturing sector, and the disruption of critical supply chains are the primary risks.
    

**Strategic Implications:** This analysis highlights three profound strategic challenges. First, there is an extremely high risk that many countries, including democracies, will invoke emergency powers to maintain domestic order, transitioning towards authoritarian governance. Second, the rationing of essential resources like food and medicine, while theoretically possible, will face severe operational difficulties due to declining state capacity and the rise of black markets. Third, an information environment rife with disinformation and incitement will accelerate social fragmentation and significantly lower the psychological threshold for violence. The post-DDC world will be an era where not only the financial order but liberal democracy and the social contract itself are fundamentally questioned.

## II. Mechanism Map: The Dynamics of Social Collapse

The causal chain from the DDC financial shock to the breakdown of social structures is characterized by multiple self-reinforcing feedback loops. This section dissects the transmission mechanisms chronologically, providing the logical foundation for subsequent analysis.

### 2.1. Initial Shock Propagation (T+0 to 30 days): From Financial Collapse to Real Economy Upheaval

The DDC is triggered by the technical failure of a U.S. Treasury auction. This signifies the disappearance of the world's "risk-free asset," instantly causing a global repricing of risk. In the L2 scenario, estimated financial asset losses range from **$12 trillion to $21.6 trillion**. The destruction of wealth on this scale immediately propagates to the real economy.

The primary transmission channel is the **"wealth effect feedback loop."** With the U.S. financial assets to GDP ratio at approximately 6:1, the evaporation of such massive wealth shatters consumer and business confidence, causing an abrupt halt in spending and investment.

This initial shock is amplified by the very regulatory framework built after the 2008 financial crisis. Specifically, the Supplementary Leverage Ratio (SLR) and margin calls from Central Counterparties (CCPs) are pro-cyclical, draining liquidity from the system precisely as market volatility spikes. This creates a self-reinforcing and catastrophic **"liquidity death spiral,"** freezing credit markets and cutting off the lifeblood to the real economy.

### 2.2. The Vicious Cycle (T+1 to 6 months): Unemployment, Austerity, and Unrest

The freeze in credit markets and evaporation of demand trigger a massive wave of layoffs. Historically, financial crises first hit credit-intensive sectors like construction and manufacturing, then ripple out to services and the public sector. Given the scale of the DDC, this process is projected to be faster and more widespread than any previous crisis.

The collapse of economic activity destroys national tax bases. With new borrowing from international markets impossible in a DDC environment, governments have no choice but to align spending with revenue. This forces a policy of **super-austerity** at the very moment social support needs are at their peak.

This situation acts as a powerful accelerator of social instability. Research by Ponticelli and Voth (2020) shows a statistically significant and strong positive correlation between the scale of government spending cuts and the frequency of social unrest events (riots, demonstrations, general strikes). Under the DDC scenario, spending cuts exceeding 5% of GDP will be unavoidable for many nations, causing the frequency of social unrest to increase non-linearly, potentially more than doubling from the baseline.

This social unrest further disrupts economic activity and increases security costs. This forces governments into further spending cuts, which in turn fuels more unrest, creating a devastating feedback loop. This is the core mechanism directly linking the financial shock to the erosion of social cohesion.

**Causal Chain Map:** DDC (Financial Shock) → Asset Value Collapse → Credit Contraction & Demand Evaporation → Mass Unemployment → Tax Revenue Collapse ↓ Increased Social Unrest ← Public Service Cuts ← Forced Austerity ↓ Further Economic Stagnation & Security Costs → Worsening Fiscal Position → (Loop)

### 2.3. Systemic Failure (T+6 to 24 months): State Dysfunction and Survival Dynamics

When the aforementioned vicious cycle crosses a critical threshold, the state begins to lose its ability to perform basic functions (maintaining order, managing infrastructure, providing social security). At this stage, to preserve its legitimacy, the government will resort to more coercive measures: invoking emergency powers and introducing rationing systems for essential resources.

Simultaneously, as the safety and opportunities provided by the state disappear, people begin to seek survival by their own means. This triggers large-scale cross-border migration and refugee flows. Domestically, black markets and underground economies expand beyond the state's control, further eroding the tax base and state legitimacy. At this stage, the crisis fully transforms from an economic one to a political and social one, questioning the very existence of the state.
