# 📘 ITIF : International Transparency-Indexed Fiat Framework

## **— Structural Issues of the Fiat Currency System and a Proposal to Introduce Transparency Benchmarks —**

(Integrated document: formal administrative brief)

---

# **1. Preface**

This document outlines the structure of the post-1971 international fiat currency system and the complementary framework **ITIF (International Transparency-Indexed Fiat Framework)** designed to enhance its transparency.

The proposal is not meant to create a new currency system or constrain monetary sovereignty. It is positioned as a **supplementary framework to improve the intelligibility and explainability of the existing fiat system**.

---

# **2. Foundations of the Current Fiat System**

Japan's 1947 Fiscal Law was designed for a gold-standard world, but since 1971 the international system has been non-convertible (fiat).

Key characteristics:

- Currency value rests on “credit”
- Taxes are a mechanism to sustain credit, not a necessary condition for money supply
- Government bonds function as private-sector financial assets, not as government debt in the gold-standard sense

This structure is already reflected in Japan’s fiscal operations.

---

# **3. Structural Challenge: Global Understanding Gap**

Fiat systems have been in operation for over 50 years, yet **there is pronounced variance in understanding even among experts**.

Notably:

- The relationship between taxation and money issuance
- The essential role of government bonds
- The concept of “credit” as the basis of currency value

For these core elements, interpretations are not unified among policymakers and researchers across countries.

This is not a matter of individual competence but a **structural limitation stemming from the absence of an absolute external standard (such as gold) in fiat systems**.

Consequently, by design it remains difficult to provide a unified explanation of the post-1971 practical and institutional fiat structure. This gap impedes policy decisions and dialogue with markets.

---

# **4. Japan’s Institutional Capability: Government-Level Understanding**

The current Government of Japan maintains an understanding of the fiat system’s structure and its link to fiscal operations that is consistent with international standards.

Specifically:

- Why fiscal expansion without tax changes is institutionally feasible
- Financial and fiscal policy operations that reflect the nature of government bonds
- The role of taxation in relation to inflation control

Japan’s policy leadership understands these points systematically and accurately, and recent official statements have remained consistent. This strength supports domestic stability and offers **an important advantage in presenting ITIF as a transparency framework in international cooperation**.

---

# **5. Purpose of ITIF: Establish a Transparency Reference Point**

The core challenge of fiat systems is that the crucial element of “credit” is not directly visible.

Accordingly, ITIF takes the following approach:

### ✔ Introduce a transparency-based metric (T-Value) rather than an external standard for currencies

T-Value assesses, in aggregate:

- Reliability of fiscal data
- Policy consistency
- Accounting transparency
- Capacity for dialogue with markets

It is a **transparency benchmark** intended to reinforce explanatory power, not to intervene in monetary sovereignty. T-Value is **non-binding, non-coercive, non-ranking, and non-intrusive to sovereignty** by design.

---

# **6. Unavoidable Institutional Challenges (Candid Disclosure)**

The candid downsides of introducing ITIF are as follows:

### ● Monetary issuance rights are matters of national sovereignty

### ● Therefore, interests will inevitably conflict when designing transparency criteria

### ● This produces an unavoidable pattern of “general agreement, specific disagreement”

This is not a defect of the framework but a necessity derived from the international principle that **currency is the ultimate layer of state sovereignty**. Stating this explicitly is expected to enhance trust in the framework’s introduction.

---

# **7. Connection to International Cooperation (BIS Alignment Potential)**

ITIF can interface naturally with:

- IMF Article IV
- OECD Fiscal Reviews
- BIS statistical transparency standards

It complements these frameworks without replacing or competing with them. It functions as a **supplementary layer** that raises transparency without infringing national systems. If Japan organizes its own understanding and offers ITIF first, there is a realistic possibility to lead the global discussion.

---

# **8. Conclusion**

ITIF is not an intervention in currency regimes; it is a **transparency framework aimed at addressing the inherent opacity of fiat systems**.

Grounded in Japan’s understanding and institutional capability, it is presented as a framework that can contribute to international cooperation.
