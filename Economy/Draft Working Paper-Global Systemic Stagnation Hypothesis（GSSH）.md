# **Draft Working Paper**

# **「Global Systemic Stagnation Hypothesis（GSSH）」**

### _— This is a draft hypothesis, not a completed theory or policy proposal._

---

# **Executive Summary**

The **Global Systemic Stagnation Hypothesis (GSSH)** is a _draft analytical framework_ intended to explain the persistent decline in decision-making capacity, socio-economic adaptability, and institutional responsiveness observed across advanced and emerging economies over the past decade.

Rather than attributing global stagnation to conventional macroeconomic drivers—such as monetary conditions, demographic trends, or productivity cycles—the GSSH proposes that **stagnation is now structurally endogenous to the information environment itself**.

Modern societies operate under:

- continuous connectivity,
    
- relentless information intake,
    
- increased cognitive load on individuals,
    
- rising administrative complexity in institutions, and
    
- rapid acceleration of exogenous shocks (technological, geopolitical, environmental).
    

GSSH argues that **decision-making systems—individual, corporate, and governmental—are experiencing a systemic compression of processing bandwidth**, resulting in:

1. **Delayed or failed policy transmission**, even when correct policy tools are available;
    
2. **Inability to construct “shared facts”** across political or social groups, leading to fragmentation;
    
3. **Reduced tolerance for ambiguity**, producing populist decision cycles and institutional brittleness;
    
4. **Chronic under-reaction and sudden over-reaction**, a hallmark of cognitive and institutional overload.
    

As a result, stagnation emerges not from a lack of opportunities, capital, or technology, but from  
**the collective erosion of society’s capacity to absorb, prioritize, and operationalize information**.

The hypothesis further suggests that:

- the diffusion of AI,
    
- the collapse of traditional information gatekeeping,
    
- the acceleration of crisis propagation across networks,
    
- and the rise of permanent global “alertness states”
    

have jointly created an environment in which **systemic overload becomes a default macro condition**.

The GSSH does **not** propose policy measures, normative claims, nor institutional redesign.  
It simply offers an analytical structure for interpreting diverse global phenomena—economic, political, technological, and social—as manifestations of a common underlying stagnation mechanism.

Future research tasks include:

- empirical measurement of cognitive-institutional load,
    
- modeling thresholds for overload-induced governance failures,
    
- and constructing early-warning indicators for systemic stagnation.
    

This paper represents an initial, incomplete draft meant to invite methodological development and interdisciplinary collaboration.
