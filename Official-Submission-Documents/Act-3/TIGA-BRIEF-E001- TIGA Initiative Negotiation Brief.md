# TIGA Initiative: A Framework for Regional Security and Stability

**Document No.:** TIGA-BRIEF-E001 **Date:** 27 July 2025 **Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL / FOR DIPLOMATIC DISCUSSION **Subject:** Proposal for a Transnational Integrated Governance Assistance (TIGA) Initiative to Counter Compound Threats in Southeast Asia

## 1. The Shared Challenge: A New Paradigm of Threat

The international community is facing a new paradigm of threat in Southeast Asia, one that transcends traditional interstate conflict. The recent military actions on the Thai-Cambodian border are not the cause of instability, but rather a symptom of a deeper, more corrosive problem: the hijacking of core state functions by sophisticated transnational criminal networks (referred to as TSCCs).

These networks have effectively compromised elements of state security apparatuses, creating a "parasitic" relationship that allows them to operate with impunity. The result is a state that acts against its own national interest, driven by the self-preservation of the criminal-state nexus. This dynamic has given rise to the "Deadman's Switch" scenario, where regional stability and global supply chains are held hostage.

## 2. The Limits of Existing Mechanisms

Current regional security frameworks and traditional diplomatic channels are ill-equipped to address this challenge.

- **Bilateral diplomacy** is ineffective when one party is not a rational state actor.
    
- **Existing regional mechanisms** are predicated on the principle of non-interference, which prevents meaningful action against threats that are internal in appearance but transnational in nature.
    

A new approach is required—one that addresses the root cause of the structural corruption without violating the core tenets of state sovereignty.

## 3. The TIGA Initiative: A Proposal for Collective Action

The Government of Japan proposes the **Transnational Integrated Governance Assistance (TIGA) Initiative** as a collaborative framework to restore sovereign integrity and regional stability. TIGA is not a military intervention, but an external support mechanism designed to act as an "immune system" for a compromised state.

The TIGA Initiative is built upon three core pillars:

### Pillar 1: Multinational Intelligence Fusion.

- **Objective:** To establish a shared, real-time intelligence picture of the TSCC's network, finances, and operations.
    
- **Action:** The creation of a joint intelligence fusion cell, staffed by personnel from partner nations, to map and analyze the threat network.
    

### Pillar 2: Coordinated Law Enforcement and Financial Sanctions.

- **Objective:** To disrupt and dismantle the TSCC's financial lifelines and operational capabilities.
    
- **Action:** Coordinated, multinational sanctions targeting key individuals and entities within the TSCC and its state-level collaborators. This includes joint anti-money laundering operations.
    

### Pillar 3: Security Sector Governance and Capacity Building.

- **Objective:** To provide long-term assistance to the affected state in reforming its security and justice sectors, thereby building resilience against future infiltration.
    
- **Action:** A comprehensive advisory and training program, led by a coalition of partners, focused on good governance, anti-corruption, and adherence to the rule of law within the military and police forces.
    

## 4. The Path Forward: A Strategic Dialogue

The complexity and sensitivity of this challenge demand a discreet and high-level approach. Japan proposes to host a closed-door **Strategic Dialogue on Regional Security** with a core group of trusted partners.

The objective of this dialogue would be to:

- Achieve a common understanding of the threat.
    
- Refine the TIGA framework and establish a multinational consensus for action.
    
- Form a dedicated working group to develop a concrete implementation plan.
    

The stability of the Indo-Pacific is a shared responsibility. We believe that through collective and decisive action under the TIGA framework, we can successfully counter this unprecedented threat and reaffirm our commitment to a rules-based international order.
